## How I read the inspiration

Jobim's signature is less the rhythm than the harmony moving under a melody that barely moves. So the A section leans on a few of his habits: the minor iv over a tonic pedal (Fmaj9 to Bbm6/F, every voice moving up a half step), a chromatic inner line descending through Am7, Abdim7, and Gm9, and a stretch where the tune sits on one note (D) while the chords change its meaning under it, from the 11th of Am7 to the 9th of C7. The bridge jumps up a minor third to Ab for the horns, then comes home through a ii–V. The tag ends on Gb7#11, the tritone sub, so the loop falls back into F the way his turnarounds do.

With no drum kit, the groove is split across the orchestra the way a bossa guitar and ganzá split it. Pizzicato basses play the thumb (root on 1, fifth on the "and" of 2 and on 3). The harp plays the finger chords in the two-bar syncopated figure. Violas pizz tick the upbeats, and clarinets anticipate each chord change by an eighth. Build-ups come at the section seams: a tremolo-strings crescendo, a timpani roll, a flute run, and a harp glissando into B and into the tutti A'. Softer harp glissandos over the tritone sub lead into A and back around the loop.

Form: Intro (4) → A, flute (16) → B, horns in Ab (8) → A', violins plus flute 8va, cellos on the chromatic line (16) → Tag (4) → loop. 132 BPM, about 87 seconds.
