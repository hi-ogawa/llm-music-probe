## Westchester Nocturne

70s CTI-style soul-jazz and fusion for a 16-track large band. It runs 48 bars at 92 BPM in 4/4, about 2:05, and loops seamlessly.

**[Open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fraw.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa%2Fllm-music-probe%2Fmain%2Fcompositions%2Fpieces%2Fwestchester-nocturne%2Fproject.json)**

### What "instrumental variation" means here

The harmony and groove stay recognizable throughout, because the same 8-bar theme sits over the same 4-bar F Dorian loop in every A section. The variation comes from three kinds of orchestration moves:

1. **The lead passes around the band.** The theme goes vibes → flute → trumpet with tenor an octave below → violins in octaves (bridge melody) → trumpet-led drop-2 brass soli with flute an octave up → vibes doubled by French horn → vibes alone.
2. **Instruments change jobs.** Vibes go from lead to background dyads, sit out, then return to lead. Flute goes lead → countermelody → octave doubling. The string ensemble plays ostinato → bridge lead → ostinato. Harpsichord is the bridge ostinato and later recolors the F-minor loop. French horn goes stabs → guide-tone pad → glue → lead double. Cello goes countermelody → guide tones → bass double.
3. **The same loop gets recolored.** One loop gets four textures: Rhodes comping, horn stabs over string ostinato, full tutti, and harpsichord broken chords. The loop also mutates harmonically (Bb13 → Bbm9, C7♯9 → Gb13).

### Harmony

- **A loop (F Dorian):** `| Fm9 | Bb13 | Ebmaj9 | Dbmaj9♯11  C7♯9 |`
- **Mutated loop:** `| Fm9 | Bbm9 | Ebmaj9 | Dbmaj9♯11  Gb13 |`. The Dorian IV13 becomes the Aeolian iv, and the dominant becomes its tritone sub. The theme's D becomes D♭ over Bbm9.
- **Bridge (A major, a chromatic mediant up a major third):** `| Amaj9 | F♯m11 | Dmaj9♯11 | C♯m7 F♯7(13) | Bm9 | E13sus | Dbmaj9♯11 | C7♯9 |`. E13sus sets up A, but it slips to Dbmaj9♯11 (the violins move E6 → F6) and C7♯9 leads back to Fm9.

### Groove

- **Swing amount, as a 16th swing (MPC-style):** 62% in the trio sections, 60% in the flute section, 58% in the big band, straight 50% in the bridge, and 56% in the shout chorus.
- **Laid-back vs pushed:** in the A sections, the snare is about 0.035 beat late, the Rhodes about 0.025 late, and the bass about 0.02 late. In the shout chorus the snare is on the grid, the bass is 0.01 early, and the horns are 0.02 early, so the peak leans forward.
- **Drum dropout:** no kick in bars 1–4 and 48, the whole kit out in bars 25–28, and silence after the hits in bars 24 and 40.

### Mix and panning plan

- **Center:** trumpet, acoustic bass, drums. **Near center:** vibes (+0.1) and flute (−0.1), because they are the other leads.
- **Left:** Rhodes −0.2, strings −0.25, tenor −0.35, French horn −0.45, cello −0.55.
- **Right:** strings pad +0.3, alto +0.35, harpsichord +0.45, trombone +0.5, congas/shaker +0.5, harp +0.6.
- **Lead on top:** in every section the lead has the highest pitch and the highest velocity. In the A sections the pad and Rhodes stay at or below A♭4, and the string ostinato sits under the trumpet. The bridge harpsichord sits under the upper violin octave. The flute counter in 29–32 stays below the violins. The only notes above the lead are the flute's octave doubling of the same melody.
