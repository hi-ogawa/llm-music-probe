# Dilla-style instrumental, 88 bpm

A loop-based beat built on call and answer. The flute states the hook, the muted trumpet answers it, and everything on top sits a little behind the beat, like a lifted sample. The empty bars on top (12, 17, 21) are part of the arrangement, so please don't fill them.

## Form

- **Intro (1-4):** Rhodes alone. In bar 3 the flute teases the first two notes of the hook (C, then a held D), and a soft choir pad leads into the drums in bar 4.
- **Verse A (5-12):** The flute plays the hook (C-D-F-A up, G-E-D-C-A down). The muted trumpet answers at the end of each four-bar phrase (7, 11). Bar 12 has nothing on top.
- **Bridge (13-20):** Ride, congas and strings come in. The phrases shrink to one bar, so the flute calls and the trumpet answers in the next bar (13/14, 18/19). Bars 15-16 belong to the flute, bar 17 is a rest, and bar 18 comes back in softer. Bar 20's E-C-C# over A7#9 hands off to the breakdown.
- **Breakdown (21-24):** Sub bass, brushes, Rhodes and a thin string line. Bar 21 has nothing on top, and the flute's C-D tease from bar 3 comes back in bar 22.
- **Verse A2 (25-32):** The 808, claps, shaker, tambourine and organ come in. The vibes carry the hook for two bars in a new colour, and then the flute returns on the bar 28 pickup and takes the C6 peak in bar 29.
- **Outro (33-36):** Everything thins out. The trumpet's last answer in bar 35 hands off to the flute's held D, which rings out to the end.

## Mix

- **Center:** all three drum kits, both basses and the choir pad. They carry the pulse and the root. The kick and snare share a kit with the hats, so the kits stay in the middle whole.
- **Call left, answer right:** the flute sits at -0.4, and so do the vibes, because the hook should always come from the same place even when its colour changes. The muted trumpet sits at +0.45, so every answer arrives from the other side.
- **Harmony:** the Rhodes is just left of center (-0.15). The sustained beds sit right (+0.3), with the strings in the bridge and breakdown and the organ in verse A2, so the comping and the pad don't stack in one spot.
- **Hand percussion** (congas in the bridge, shaker and tambourine in verse A2) sits at -0.25, on the hook side. When the flute rests and the trumpet answers from the right, the percussion keeps the left side moving, so the mix doesn't tip right on every answer.
- Panning is equal-power, so no levels were changed to compensate for it.
