## How I read the inspiration

Dilla beats are a looped soul chord progression played against a groove that is deliberately off the grid. The snare drags late, the kick lands a hair early and in a different place every bar, and the hats swing somewhere between straight and triplet, so the whole beat feels like it is leaning back. With no drum kit, I gave each drum role to an orchestra part. Low pizzicato basses are the kick, with timpani doubling them in the last section. A pizzicato chord that lands about a 16th-of-a-beat late is the snare. High pizzicato violins playing swung, half-ghosted 16ths are the hats. String ensemble stabs at uneven spots in the bar stand in for the chopped sample.

The main loop is a IV–iii–ii–V in Db (Gbmaj9, Fm7, Ebm9, Ab13), and the melodies sit behind the beat too. The form runs like this:

- **A (bars 1–8):** a dusty intro with pad and harp, while the celesta hints at the hook.
- **B (bars 9–24):** the beat drops. The flute takes the hook, and then the horns come in.
- **C (bars 25–32):** a Dilla-style flip into Bb minor (Bbm9, Gbmaj7#11, Ebm9, F7b9). The groove goes half-time with triplet hats, and a muted trumpet leads.
- **D (bars 33–40):** a tutti return with the violins on the hook, then a breakdown back into the intro so the loop closes.

Every section boundary gets a two-bar build: a timpani roll, tremolo strings, a brass crescendo, and a violin run. The orchestra then cuts out for half a beat before the downbeat hits, which is the beat-tape dropout move.
