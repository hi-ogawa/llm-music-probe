A fresh 4-bar guitar loop rendered offline with a better electric guitar SoundFont, split off from the Night Drive in D♭ cutting guitar iteration (https://gist.github.com/hi-ogawa-agent/348fb1c97dfb6be73254543ce1714081). Context and plan: https://github.com/hi-ogawa/toy-midi/issues/861 ("Next experiment: an electric guitar font").

Each version is a full project file here (`vN-<slug>.json`) with a Toy MIDI link, for reviewing the notes on the piano roll. Toy MIDI can't load another SoundFont yet (#852), so it plays GeneralUser. The FSBS renders are posted as MP3s in the Discord thread, not kept here.

## Why a fresh loop

The question in #861 is whether a better sample fixes electric guitar compared with GeneralUser program 27. On Night Drive, FreePats FSBS clean beat program 27 by ear (Night Drive iteration 1). Iteration 2 there tried stroke simulation on the existing part, which drifted into tuning one arrangement. This loop starts from scratch with guitar-idiomatic writing, to hear what the FSBS sample can carry across two different guitar roles: chord cutting and a single-note line.

## Setup

- Font: FreePats [FSBS Electric Guitar Clean](https://freepats.zenvoid.org/ElectricGuitar/clean-electric-guitar.html), CC0, the full 129 MiB version. It has a single preset and no mute samples.
- `loop.mjs`: `node loop.mjs <out project.json> [--fsbs] [--reps N]`. Without `--fsbs` it writes a plain Toy MIDI project on GeneralUser, with the muted chks on program 28 (muted guitar). With `--fsbs`, the guitar tracks get the FSBS font and a release offset each. The MP3s use `--fsbs --reps 4`.
- Rendering uses `render.mjs` from the Night Drive gist: `node render.mjs <project.json> --sf2 <GeneralUser-GS.sf2> --out <dir>`. It renders each track with fluidsynth, applies gain and pan, mixes, and prints per-track LUFS. A track's `sf2` field overrides the font, and its `nrpn` field offsets a SoundFont generator through SoundFont NRPN, such as `{ "38": -2800 }` for the volume release.

## Faking a muted stroke on FSBS

FSBS ignores short notes. Its release takes about 250 ms to fall 20 dB and 425 ms to fall 40 dB whether a note lasts 11 ms or 44 ms (measured on Night Drive), so shortening notes alone can't mute anything. A release offset can. `nrpn: { 38: -2800 }` brings a 22 ms note down 40 dB in about 45 ms, which is a believable "chk", and `-1000` in about 165 ms, which sounds like a choked chord. So each guitar part with a muted texture uses two channels with the same sample: one for open strokes, and one for muted strokes with 22 ms notes and the short release.

This trick first appeared in Night Drive iteration 2, which never got a verdict by ear. This loop is the first time it was heard and judged.

## Iteration

1. **Two interlocking guitars, written from the fretboard** (`v1-interlock.json`, [open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fgist.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa-agent%2Fadda447fab1379e354214a37cc9cd357%2Fraw%2Fv1-interlock.json)). An E dorian vamp, Em9 to A13, at 104 BPM.
   - Left, cutting (panned -0.6): a top-string grip, 7-7-7 on G-B-e (D F# B). A13 drops the G-string finger to fret 6 (C# F# B), the one-finger move of the funk vamp. Each bar is a 16th grid of open accents, muted chks, and air strokes, where the hand moves without hitting the strings. Downstrokes on even 16ths roll low to high and add the D string under muted strokes, and upstrokes roll high to low on the top two strings. Accents choke at a 16th, except the last one in each bar, which rings for an 8th. A few accents slide in from a half step below as grace chords (dropped, see below).
   - Right, single-note line (panned +0.6): a 16th riff on the A, D, and G strings with dead notes, mostly filling the cutting part's gaps. Its notes last a 16th or an 8th, with a release offset of -1600.
   - Finger bass and drums on GeneralUser for context.
   - Humanizing: ±3–4 ms timing jitter, velocity jitter, upstrokes about 5 ms late, and a velocity taper across each stroke.

   Feedback: the chord cutting is quite good. It's the first time guitar nuance came through well enough. The lead line feels awkward, more like a synth played on keys than a guitar. **Kept**: the cutting part. **Open**: the lead line.

   Later feedback: the bar boundaries 2–3, 3–4, and 4–1 have an odd, arpeggio-like stutter. Those are exactly the boundaries with a slide grace chord. The grace was my improvisation, not a known MIDI technique: a full 3-string chord a half step below, rolled like a real strum, only about 20 velocity under the target and 45 ms early, so it reads as a second picked chord. A real chord slide is one attack followed by a pitch glide, which in MIDI means pitch bend (as #861 cites from Producer Society). **Dropped**: the grace-chord slides. Also found: bar 3's anticipation on the `a` of 4 plays Em9 and rings into the A13 bar, where the next chord belongs.

   The good cutting result can't be credited to the faked mute or the sample alone. The guitar's backing phrase itself may be the main reason: a funk cutting pattern on a one-finger Em9 to A13 vamp is the most guitar-native comping phrase there is, while Night Drive's cutting is a city pop part on a moving progression. The loop also has other writing changes compared with Night Drive (air strokes, syncopated accents, and slides).

   My guess at the lead's problem, not yet tested: every note gets a fresh pick attack and stops cleanly at a 16th or 8th, there's no pitch movement (slides, hammer-ons, pull-offs, vibrato), and the shortened release cuts notes off the way a key release would. #861 already expected expressive lead lines to be the hardest case.
2. **No slides, fixed anticipation** (`v2-no-slides.json`, [open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fgist.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa-agent%2Fadda447fab1379e354214a37cc9cd357%2Fraw%2Fv2-no-slides.json)). Same as v1 with the grace-chord slides removed, and the accent on the last 16th of bar 3 now plays the next bar's A13. Feedback: the cutting works, and even Toy MIDI's GeneralUser playback (chks on program 28) feels like guitar. So the writing, not the font, was the main factor for cutting, and Night Drive's problem is likely its guitar phrasing and arrangement. **Kept**: the cutting grid as the model to port back to Night Drive.
3. **Bass down an octave** (`v3-bass-octave.json`, [open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fgist.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa-agent%2Fadda447fab1379e354214a37cc9cd357%2Fraw%2Fv3-bass-octave.json)). v1 and v2 wrote the bass from E2 to A3, an octave above a real bass, because I took E2 for the open E string, which is E1 (MIDI 28). Not intended. Now E1 to A2. Feedback: the octave-up bass made v1 and v2 sound like a three-guitar jam, which almost read as a design choice, but the dropped octave is simply easier to play bass over. **Kept**: v3.
4. **Lead articulation through pitch bend** (`v4-lead-articulation.json`, [open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fgist.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa-agent%2Fadda447fab1379e354214a37cc9cd357%2Fraw%2Fv4-lead-articulation.json), which plays the notes without the articulations). The #861 pitch-bend experiment, on the lead line only. Notes carry `art: "h"` or `"p"` on the target of a hammer-on or pull-off, `vib` (cents) on held notes, and `curl` (cents). `render.mjs` expands them into channel pitch bend with a ±12 semitone range: a hammer-on or pull-off extends the previous note and steps the bend, with no new note-on, and the bend resets before the next pick. The legato spots: G3-A3 (D string 5-7) in bars 1, 3, and 4, B3-D4 (G string 4-7) at the end of bars 1 and 3, C#4-D4 in bars 2 and 4, and pull-offs D4-C#4 and D4-B3 at the end of bars 2 and 4. All three versions drop the shortened release, so notes ring naturally.
   - A: picked, as before but with the natural release.
   - B: A plus the hammer-ons and pull-offs.
   - C: B plus vibrato (25 cents, 5.5 Hz, after 120 ms) on the held 8th notes, and a 50-cent curl on bar 2's held E4.

   Rendered on FSBS as lead solo and in the loop for A, B, and C. Feedback: it all sounds almost picked. The bends work (measured pitch: G3 196 Hz to A3 220 Hz with no new attack), but the articulations aren't meaningful, because they were placed by a rule (any adjacent stepwise 16ths) instead of by phrasing. Also, with the shortened release removed, the line's 20 ms dead notes rang for about 250 ms as pitched notes, so the line gained picked notes instead of ghosts. **Dropped**: rule-placed articulation.
5. **Phrased articulation, real dead notes** (`v5-lead-phrased.json`, [open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fgist.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa-agent%2Fadda447fab1379e354214a37cc9cd357%2Fraw%2Fv5-lead-phrased.json)). The dead notes move to their own muted channel with the short release (GeneralUser program 28 in Toy MIDI). The articulation follows the phrase: picks on the notes the groove leans on, legato for the notes between them. Bar 1 picks E3 on the push and hammers through G3 and A3 into the snare, with vibrato on the held A3, then B3 hammered to D4 into the next downbeat. Bar 2 picks D4 on the snare and bends it to E4 (the 5th of A), releases to D4, and pulls off to C#4: one pick, four pitches. Bar 4 ends with a pull-off from D4 to B3 that falls into the E3 downbeat. The renderer gained `art: "b"` (pick below, bend up) and `"r"` (glide back), checked by measured pitch. Rendered on FSBS, picked (P) against phrased (D), solo and in the loop. Feedback: it wasn't clear what had changed. Hiroshi wanted hammer-ons judged first, as the easiest articulation to hear, with bends left for later. **Deferred**: bends.
6. **Hammer-ons and pull-offs only** (`v6-lead-legato.json`, [open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fgist.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa-agent%2Fadda447fab1379e354214a37cc9cd357%2Fraw%2Fv6-lead-legato.json)). Hammer-ons first, because they're the easiest articulation to judge by ear, so v5 without the bend and vibrato. Bends come later. Bar 2 now picks E4 on the snare and pulls off to D4 and C#4 (G string 9-7-6), one pick for three pitches. 24 legato notes per two passes, dead notes on the muted channel. Rendered on FSBS, solo and in the loop, to compare with v5's picked render (P). Feedback: still unclear what changed across v4–v6, which piled up variants instead of isolating one idea. **Dropped** for a reset (v7).
7. **Reset: 16th mutes only** (`v7-lead-16th-mutes.json`, [open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fgist.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa-agent%2Fadda447fab1379e354214a37cc9cd357%2Fraw%2Fv7-lead-16th-mutes.json)). The legato variants (v4–v6) left it unclear what changed, so this resets to the cutting idea alone: the picking hand keeps moving in 16ths, and every 16th is either a fretted note or a muted ghost. Rests become ghosts, the dead notes become real mutes (GeneralUser program 28 in Toy MIDI, FSBS with the short release in the render), and fretted notes choke at a 16th like the cutting accents. No legato, bends, or vibrato. The ghosts first came out 21 LU under the fretted notes, which is likely inaudible, so they were raised 10 dB to about 11 LU under, close to the cutting part's ratio. The lead also came out about 3 LU under the cutting part, a side effect of choking its notes, so both lead channels were raised 4 dB to sit level with the cutting (lead-M3-loop). Rendered on FSBS, solo and in the loop. Feedback: a good step, with a better guitar feel. The hammer-ons still didn't come through, which pointed at FSBS's weak attack rather than the writing. **Kept**: the 16th-mute line.
8. **All guitars on the FlameStudios Telecaster** (`v8-tele.json`, [open in Toy MIDI](https://toy-midi.hiro18181.workers.dev/draft?src=https%3A%2F%2Fgist.githubusercontent.com%2Fhi-ogawa-agent%2Fadda447fab1379e354214a37cc9cd357%2Fraw%2Fv8-tele.json)). After a font comparison on the lead (logged in #861) found FSBS's pick attack the weakest, every guitar channel moves to the [FlameStudios Fender Telecaster Bridge Pickup Direct In](https://github.com/gdm-2112/electric-guitar-soundfonts/tree/main/SF3/FlameStudios) SF3 (GPL): cutting accents and lead notes on its Standard preset (choke release offset -1000), cutting chks and lead ghosts on its Muted preset, which has real muted recordings, so no faked mute. The lead is v7's 16th-mute line plus the phrased hammer-ons and pull-offs (pitch bend). Balanced to cutting and lead at -31 LUFS each, mutes at -42. Feedback: this is good. The hammer-ons now read as legato, so on a font with a sharp attack, pitch-bend legato works for a funky 16th lead. **Kept**: v8.
9. **Pickup and guitar comparison** (same notes as v8, so no new project file). The loop rendered on each of the five FlameStudios direct-in files: Telecaster bridge and neck, Les Paul bridge and neck, and Jaguar with both pickups, each with its Standard preset for open notes and its Muted preset for mutes. Bridge pickups sound bright and tight, neck pickups warm. Then a two-guitar pair: Telecaster bridge for the cutting and Les Paul bridge for the lead. Feedback: the warmer pickups suit the lead (Telecaster neck, Les Paul bridge), and these variants are enough for experiments. As one clean default, the Telecaster neck. As a pair, Telecaster bridge cutting with Les Paul bridge lead. Velocities 0–45 hit the Muted recording layer in these files, so open notes need velocities above 45. Verdict recorded in #861.
