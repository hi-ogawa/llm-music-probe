How to read

  • Layers. Genres sit on top. Below them, areas stack the way a track is built: Notes (harmony, voicing, melody), Time (rhythm, drums, bass), Shape (form, arranging), and Sound (synthesis, sampling, mixing, voice).
  • Color is roots, the tradition a technique comes from: Classical and film Jazz, soul, and gospel Pop and songwriting Electronic, hip-hop, and production Latin and African diaspora Universal. The thin bar under each area title shows its mix. Click a legend entry to show only that tradition.
  • Lenses. Click a genre to light up the techniques it uses across every area. Click a technique to see what it is and which genres use it. Esc clears.
  • Can toy-midi play it? switches to a demo view: plain chips can be demoed now, dashed ones only approximately, and hatched ones not yet. The pills rank what toy-midi is missing, and clicking one lights up what it blocks.
  • Roots tags and genre links are judgment calls, and one root per technique is a simplification.

toy-midi

A side note for picking demos. Checked against https://github.com/hi-ogawa/toy-midi main at 8da9151 (#810). Notes carry pitch, start, duration, and velocity. Tracks choose a GM preset or one of 9 drum kits (Standard, Room, Power, Electronic, TR-808, Jazz, Brush, Orchestra, SFX) from the bundled soundfont, and have static gain, pan, mute, solo, and up to 8 peaking EQ bands. Audio clips can be imported, moved, trimmed, and gained.

Missing What that means
Pitch bend Pitch bend, glide, portamento
Pedal and swells Sustain pedal, mod wheel, expression and volume swells (only per-note velocity exists)
Tempo changes Tempo or meter changes within a project (one tempo, one time signature)
Automation Automation of any mix or sound parameter over time
Effects Time and modulation effects such as reverb, delay, chorus, and sends
Dynamics Dynamics and saturation such as compression, sidechain, limiting, and distortion (no buses either)
Filters Low-pass, high-pass, shelf, and resonant filters (track EQ is peaking-only)
Synth design Designing a patch instead of picking a GM preset
Audio sampling Slicing, pitching, stretching, or reversing audio
Vocals Sung or rapped vocals and lyrics
